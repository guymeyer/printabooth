3

LED channel

▶
Strip starts at the top of

The strip turns two corners in the plane of the face, so it has to bend sideways with its LEDs facing forward: a zig-zag (S-shape) strip lying flat, or a side-light strip standing on its edge. Ordinary flat and COB strips only bend the other way; with those, cut at each corner and solder a short jumper. Picking a strip sizes the channel for it: 0.5 mm each side, and enough depth for the diffuser plus an air gap that blends the LEDs into a line. The channel is one unbroken U around the sides and bottom of the face; it stops at the shoulders, under the cassette's accent band, so it never crosses the cassette slot and the front lip stays 2 mm thin. One strip lights all of it: it starts at the top of the side you pick, where its lead presses into a slot that runs straight back into the box, and you cut off what's left at the other shoulder.

Back panel & threads

▶
Holes

Wall mount: the back is a plain wall plate with two keyholes, so it has no accessory holes.

Sized for Thread Boards' 12 mm threaded screws on a 25 mm grid. Thread Boards doesn't publish its thread, so this is an estimate: print the thread test coupon (in Export) and try one of your screws before the full panel. Loose or tight? Change the clearance; wrong size? Measure the screw's outside diameter and the distance between crests and type them here.

Seen from behind the booth · click or drag to toggle

The back is solid and screws on at all eight lug points, so one back fits any ring count. Hang heavy items like a battery low, or strap them to a ring floor.

Mount & height

▶
Mount
Plywood layers

    The front frame, cassette and LED channel are the same as the stand. Behind them a shallow case and a flat wall plate replace the rings and back panel. The cable leaves through a notch in the bottom by the wall, or through the hole in the plate if it comes out of the wall.

    One leg points back so a push on the screen meets the strong side of the tripod. The base is a plain drum centered under the head; each leg threads into a socket bored into its underside, with a seat square to the leg so the ferrule sits flat. The power cable drops through the center of the drum.

    PLA softens above about 55 °C. Not recommended for hot cars, direct sun or outdoor summer events. Use PETG for those.

    Shell geometry

    Slicer settings

    Material

    The nozzle reshapes the shell: walls snap to whole extrusion widths and skins to whole layers, so they print as full perimeters with no gap fill. Outer size and every fit stay the same. The material only changes the slicer settings, and any brand works: the projects use PrusaSlicer's Generic PETG or PLA preset. The front frame and rings each get a shallow V-groove down their underside: they print with it at the back of the bed, where the Rear seam lines up in it.
