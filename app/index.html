Shell joints

The cassette slides straight down into the shell; there's no cap and nothing to unscrew. Its top edge sits flush with the shell. A rounded finger scoop straddles the seam and continues into a small recessed ridge on the back of the cassette, so your fingertips hook under it to lift it out.

LED channel

▶

Match the width to your strip plus 1 mm; the 6 mm default takes a 5 mm COB strip. Depth is the air gap that softens hotspots. The channel is a U around the sides and bottom of the face and stops at the shoulders, under the cassette's accent band, so it never crosses the cassette slot and the front lip stays 2 mm thin. Two strips start under the solid bottom cover, where their leads drop into a pocket that opens into the box, and run up each side.

Legs & height

▶

One leg points back so a push on the screen meets the strong side of the tripod. The base is a plain drum centered under the head; each leg threads into a socket bored into its underside, with a seat square to the leg so the ferrule sits flat. The power cable drops through the center of the drum.

PLA softens above about 55 °C. Not recommended for hot cars, direct sun or outdoor summer events. Use PETG for those.

Shell geometry

Slicer settings

Material

The nozzle reshapes the shell: walls snap to whole extrusion widths and skins to whole layers, so they print as full perimeters with no gap fill. Outer size and every fit stay the same. The material only changes the slicer settings, and any brand works: the projects use PrusaSlicer's Generic PETG or PLA preset. The front frame and rings each get a shallow V-groove down their underside: they print with it at the back of the bed, where the Rear seam lines up in it.
